01 · The role

Principal AI & Transformation Expert

Mission: get every department working with AI within 12 months. The role sits at headquarters, between business and IT, and delivers through other people rather than building alone.

THE ROLE OWNS
  • The use-case funnel: identify → evaluate → implement
  • Change management, enablement & training
  • Guardrails, champion network & round table
BUSINESS OWNS
  • Use cases, data & domain knowledge
  • The pilot goal and its metric
  • Adoption: the story is theirs
IT OWNS
  • Platform, integration & security
  • Approved tool operation
  • Data access & architecture

Think of it as air traffic control. The role does not fly the planes. It decides what takes off, in what order, and keeps the runways clear. Business says what it needs, IT builds it safely, the role keeps both moving. A service manager in character, never a gatekeeper.

02 · The 12 months

Four phases, one ticket

M1–2

01 IDENTIFY

Walk through every department with IT. Ask what wastes their week. No tech talk yet. You leave with a list of real problems, and with the AI tools people already use in secret.

M3–5

02 EVALUATE & PILOT

Every idea goes through the same short pre-flight checklist. Three pilots take off, 90 days each, one number to prove. Champions named, guardrails published.

M6–9

One hour a month to decide what flies next. Training runs. What worked becomes a recipe other teams can copy.

M10–12

04 ANCHOR

Every department plan carries an AI goal. A light governance board. The ideas now come from the teams.

03 · Approaching departments

The conversation playbook: pain first, tool later

  • 01Listen: "What eats your week?" Sit in their meetings, walk the line. Never open with AI.
  • 02Show: two weeks later, a 45-minute demo on their own data. Watching their own quote get drafted beats any slide.
  • 03Co-create: pilot built with their people, one measurable goal, 90 days. They name it.
  • 04Hand over: the champion presents the result to leadership. You sit in the audience.

Do

Speak their KPIs (scrap rate, OTD, quote turnaround) · celebrate small wins loudly · make trying safe.

Don't

Lead with tools or hype · promise headcount savings · centralise every decision · punish failed experiments.

04 · Culture mechanics

How the mindset actually shifts

Champions network

One volunteer per department, 20% protected time, monthly exchange. They translate. You don't scale, they do.

Visible quick wins

Every pilot ≤ 90 days with one number attached. Wins are shown at town halls by the team, not the AI lead.

Safe to try

Weekly open "AI hour", sandbox with approved tools, no-blame reviews of failed experiments. Failure is data.

Small wins build trust faster than big plans. Rule of thumb: after month 6, more ideas should come from the departments than from the AI lead. That is the signal the culture is moving.

05 · Guardrails

Brakes let you drive fast

You do not put brakes on a car to make it slower. You put them on so it can go fast. Rules on data, tools and risk work the same way. And the real risk is not the technology. It is someone pasting a customer drawing into a free chatbot. So the question flips: not is AI safe, but is our data safe from AI.

  • Most daily use is low risk: copilots, drafting, inspection support. Say clearly when something is AI, and you are largely done.
  • One narrow high-risk lane: anything that screens people, like CV sorting, is high risk under EU law from December 2027. Build it so a human decides, starting now.
  • Three documents cover most of it: a short AI policy, a list of approved tools, and training per role. Training is already a legal duty.
  • One register, every system: purpose, owner, risk level, data used. No entry, no rollout.
06 · Measuring

KPIs that prove mindset, not just usage

ADOPTION

% of staff using approved AI tools weekly. Target: 60% by M12.

CAPABILITY

% trained per role tier. Target: 80% basics, 100% of champions advanced.

VALUE

At least one live use case per department, plus hours saved or quality gained per pilot.

MOMENTUM

Bottom-up ideas per quarter: the culture metric. Should overtake top-down by M6.

A bakery knows what a single croissant costs to make. We should know what one AI-drafted quote costs against doing it by hand. If AI is not cheaper or better, we stop and say so. Reported monthly, on one page. No dashboards nobody reads.
