AI Governance · Europe · August 2026

How to Govern AI in a European Company, Right Now

Best practices and legal obligations under the EU AI Act, the Digital Omnibus, and the adjacent EU digital rulebook.

01 · Why now

August 2026 is not a drill

Live
2 Aug 2026

The AI Act's general applicability date. Article 50 transparency duties apply, and the Commission's enforcement powers over general-purpose AI (GPAI) providers activate.

Fines
€35M / 7%

Maximum penalty for prohibited AI practices, banned since Feb 2025. Other violations: up to €15M or 3% of global turnover.

Relief
Dec 2027

The Digital Omnibus deferred most high-risk obligations from Aug 2026 to 2 Dec 2027 (Annex III) and 2 Aug 2028 (Annex I products). Breathing room, not a pardon.

Everything else (GDPR, DORA, NIS2, the Data Act) still applies to your AI systems on top.

01 · The legal landscape

EU AI Act: where we are on the timeline

1 Aug 2024

Entry into force. The clock starts.

2 Feb 2025

Prohibitions + AI literacy (Art. 4). Social scoring, manipulative AI, most real-time biometric ID banned. Staff must be adequately AI-literate.

2 Aug 2025

GPAI obligations + governance. Model providers owe documentation, copyright policy, training-data summaries. National authorities designated.

2 Aug 2026

General applicability. Transparency (Art. 50): disclose chatbots, label AI-generated content and deepfakes. GPAI enforcement powers and fines go live.

2 Dec 2027

High-risk, Annex III (HR, credit, education, critical infrastructure…), deferred by the Digital Omnibus from Aug 2026.

2 Aug 2028

High-risk, Annex I: AI embedded in regulated products (medical devices, machinery, vehicles).

Most companies are deployers, not providers, so lighter duties, but fine-tuning or white-labelling a model can flip you into the provider role.

01 · The legal landscape

What the 2026 Digital Omnibus actually changed

  • High-risk deadlines deferred: Annex III to 2 Dec 2027; Annex I products to 2 Aug 2028. Approved by Parliament (16 June) and Council (29 June 2026).
  • SME & small mid-cap relief: simplified procedures and lower fines for companies under ~750 employees / €150M turnover.
  • GDPR opening for bias testing: special-category data may be processed to detect and correct bias, under strict necessity, pseudonymisation and deletion safeguards.
  • Simplification, not deregulation: prohibitions, transparency and GPAI enforcement were not delayed. Plan against the new dates; don't bet on further slippage.
01 · The legal landscape

The AI Act is one layer: the stack is bigger

GDPR

Legal basis for training and inference data, DPIAs for high-risk processing, automated-decision rights (Art. 22). Your AI governance must be joined to your privacy programme, not parallel to it.

DORA & NIS2

Financial entities and essential services must fold AI vendors into ICT third-party risk management and incident reporting. An LLM API is a critical ICT service.

Data Act & Data Governance Act

Access, sharing and switching rules for the data that feeds your models.

Copyright & DSA

TDM opt-outs constrain training data; platforms face systemic-risk duties for AI-driven features.

02 · Best practices

Don't invent a framework: compose one

FrameworkRoleNature
EU AI ActThe legal floor for any EU market exposureBinding law, financial penalties
ISO/IEC 42001Certifiable AI management system (AIMS) that proves governance to customers and auditorsVoluntary, certifiable
NIST AI RMFOperational risk vocabulary: Govern · Map · Measure · ManageVoluntary guidance
OECD principlesEthical baseline referenced by regulatorsPrinciples

The common 2026 pattern: one cross-framework register per AI system, mapping each control once to AI Act articles, ISO 42001 clauses and NIST functions, instead of three parallel compliance programmes.

02 · Best practices

Operating model: central rules, federated ownership

Decide

AI Governance Board

Cross-functional (legal, security, data, business, ethics). Owns policy, risk appetite and the exception process. Small, mandated, decision-only meetings.

Enable

Platform / CoE

Turns policy into paved roads: approved model catalogue, templates pre-wired with logging, guardrails and cost controls. Supplies; doesn't gatekeep.

Own

Product teams

Accountable for each use case: purpose, risk classification, budget, human oversight. Your system, your risk, your name in the register.

The anti-pattern: a central committee approving every use case. It becomes the bottleneck, and shadow AI routes around it.

02 · Best practices

The AI register is the spine of everything

You cannot classify, monitor or defend a system you don't know you're running.

  • Inventory every AI system: including SaaS embedded AI, browser copilots and that "experiment" finance runs on a personal API key.
  • Record per system: purpose, owner, risk tier, legal basis, data used, model/provider, human-oversight design, budget.
  • Gate at intake: no register entry, no deployment, no spend. Wire the register into procurement and the cloud vending process.
  • Review on change: model swap, new data, new audience → re-classify.
02 · Best practices

Shift governance left: into design, not after deployment

  • 01Intake & classification: risk-tier and DPIA screening at the idea stage, in the same form that requests budget.
  • 02Design controls: data governance, bias testing (now GDPR-permitted under safeguards), human-in-the-loop points, red-team plan.
  • 03Pre-launch gate: transparency notices, content labelling, logging switched on, fallback path defined.
  • 04Run: monitor drift, incidents, cost per outcome; feed serious incidents into your reporting duties.
  • 05Retire: decommission dates by default, especially sandboxes and agent experiments.
02 · Best practices

AI literacy is a legal duty and your cheapest control

What Article 4 requires

Since Feb 2025, providers and deployers must ensure staff operating AI have a sufficient level of AI literacy, proportionate to context and the people affected.

What good looks like

Role-based training: builders get evaluation and red-teaming; users get prompts, limits and escalation; leadership gets risk and accountability. Track completion, it's evidence.

Trained people are also the control that catches what tooling misses: hallucinated citations, leaked data in prompts, quiet scope creep from assistant to decision-maker.

The 90-Day Plan

A realistic sequence for a company starting (or restarting) today.

03 · The plan

First 90 days

Days 1–30

See

Stand up the governance board. Build the AI register from cloud bills, SSO logs and a team survey. Screen for prohibited practices and unlabelled chatbots/content: those are live exposures.

Days 31–60

Sort

Risk-classify everything found. Fix Article 50 transparency gaps. Map which systems will be high-risk in Dec 2027 and open a workplan per system. Launch role-based AI literacy training.

Days 61–90

Systematise

Wire the register into intake and procurement. Publish the approved-model catalogue and paved-road templates. Choose the framework spine (ISO 42001 as target, NIST as method) and baseline against it.

Then: quarterly board reviews, an annual conformity dry-run for future high-risk systems, and continuous monitoring as business-as-usual.

04 · Takeaways

Five things to remember

  • 1Transparency and GPAI enforcement are live now: the Omnibus delayed high-risk duties, nothing else.
  • 2Deadlines moved; the direction didn't: use Dec 2027 to build properly, not to wait.
  • 3The register is the spine: inventory first, everything else attaches to it.
  • 4Central rules, federated ownership: paved roads beat approval committees.
  • 5Compose frameworks: AI Act as floor, ISO 42001 as system, NIST as method: one control set, mapped three ways.
